# Draft a relevant conversation

Use the sender's actual offer and supported account findings. Keep every factual personalization tied to an evidence record. If the connection between the finding and offer is weak, return the account to review rather than inventing a business problem.

Write one short initial email and two follow-ups in the brief's language. Lead with the account's specific problem or opportunity, explain the relevant offer, and end with one concrete next step. Use a clear subject. Keep the initial message roughly 70–120 words when the user's brief permits; follow-ups should be shorter and add a useful detail rather than fake urgency. Adapt length when the context demands it.

Use direct, human language. Keep internal validation explanations and qualification notes in the account brief rather than padding the recipient's email with disclaimers. State the supported observation and proposed next step without implying that an untested problem or result is established.

Do not invent a relationship, pretend to have used the product, claim unmeasured losses or growth, imply a guaranteed result, or offer work the sender has not authorized. Use real proof points only when the sender supplied supporting material. Respect the user's contact preferences and suppression list.

Store the subject, initial body, exactly two follow-up bodies, contact ID (or null), and evidence IDs. Follow-ups inherit the draft's evidence requirement: add any new factual finding to the cited records. Missing recipient email can still yield a review draft, clearly marked `needs_contact`.

The exporter withholds message bodies for suppressed accounts, unqualified companies, conflicting evidence cited by the draft, or cited AI findings without provider/model/date. An unrelated unresolved finding does not block a website-based draft. If a conflict changes whether the company qualifies at all, return the company to `review`. A published recipient only produces `review_ready`, never a guarantee of inbox placement or permission to send.

Read the rendered outreach before handing it over. Check names, roles, source support, whether the offer addresses the observation, and whether each CTA is an action the recipient can understand. The helper checks structure, not persuasiveness, truth, or the legality of a future campaign. Do not send or schedule messages through this skill.
